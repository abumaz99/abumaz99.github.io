Nomi privacy notice

Last updated: 29 September 2026

Who is responsible

Abu Mazhar (London, United Kingdom) is the data controller for Nomi. Contact: abumaz99@hotmail.com.

What the app handles

Nomi can store daily steps, sleep duration, mood, energy, stress, tags, optional private notes, and consent records on your device. An account is optional. Version 1 does not collect raw health samples, heart rate, location, contacts, microphone recordings, or advertising identifiers, and does not offer in-app AI coaching.

Your choices

Health access, optional reminders, an account, and cloud backup are separate and optional. Declining any of them does not prevent local check-ins. You can withdraw cloud consent in Settings.

Health and cloud processing

Health information is read from Apple HealthKit (or Android Health Connect where available) with read-only permission and reduced to daily aggregates. With explicit cloud consent and a signed-in account, those aggregates and check-ins may be stored in an EU-hosted Supabase project under UK GDPR and related data-protection law, for providing the service you requested.

Sharing

Data is not sold. Health data is not used for advertising or marketing profiling. Processors are limited to essential hosting and security providers needed to run the service (including Supabase when cloud backup is enabled). No AI coaching processor is used in version 1 of the client.

Retention and security

Local data remains until you delete it or uninstall, subject to device backup behaviour. Cloud data remains until you delete your account or request erasure. Controls include encryption in transit, encrypted platform storage, row-level access rules, and data-minimised logs.

Your rights

Depending on UK data-protection law, you may request access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, or object to processing, and may complain to the ICO. Use the in-app export and deletion controls, or email abumaz99@hotmail.com.

Safety and scope

Nomi is general wellbeing software, not a medical service. It does not diagnose or provide crisis counselling. In immediate danger call 999 or go to A&E. Samaritans are available on 116 123.
